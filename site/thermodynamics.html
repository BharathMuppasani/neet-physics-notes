Thermodynamics

1 Core

State variables and the first law

A thermodynamic state is specified by equilibrium variables such as pressure, volume and temperature. Internal energy is a state function: its change depends only on the endpoints. Heat and work describe transfers along a process and depend on its path.

We take heat Q positive into the gas and work W positive when done by the gas. Energy conservation then says that input heat increases internal energy or supplies outward work. Compression gives negative gas work under this convention.

State variables and the first law
\[ \Delta U=Q-W,\qquad \Delta U=nC_V\Delta T\quad\text{(ideal gas, constant }C_V) \]
Symbols and assumptions: ΔU internal-energy change (J); Q heat added (J); W work done by gas (J); n gas amount (mol); C_V molar constant-volume heat capacity (J/mol K); ΔT temperature change (K). Ideal-gas U depends only on T; capacities can vary with temperature.
Watch the assumption

Do not mix the work-by-gas sign convention with a formula written for work-on-gas.

Worked example

A gas receives 500 J heat and does 200 J work. Find internal-energy change.

Show solution

ΔU=500−200=300 J. The gas retains part of the supplied energy and transfers the rest as work.

Check the idea

2 Core

Pressure–volume work and paths

A gas does work when it moves a piston against external pressure. For a quasistatic reversible process, the gas pressure nearly matches the external pressure and the work is the area under the gas p–V curve.

Expansion gives positive work, compression negative work. Two processes connecting the same endpoints can enclose different areas and therefore have different W and Q while sharing the same ΔU. In a general irreversible process, use external resisting pressure for boundary work.

Pressure–volume work and paths
\[ W=\int_{V_i}^{V_f}p_{\rm ext}\,dV,\quad W=p(V_f-V_i)\quad\text{(constant pressure)} \]
Symbols and assumptions: W work by gas (J); p_ext external resisting pressure (Pa); V_i/V_f initial/final volumes (m³); p constant pressure. A reversible quasistatic path permits p_ext≈p_gas; gas p–V area is not automatically valid for arbitrary irreversible paths.
Watch the assumption

A litre is 10⁻³ m³; pressure–volume area then has joule units.

Worked example

A gas expands from 2 to 5 litres against constant 10⁵ Pa. Find work.

Show solution

ΔV=3×10⁻³ m³, so W=10⁵×3×10⁻³=300 J.

Check the idea

3 Core

Isochoric and isobaric heating

In an isochoric process volume stays fixed, so no expansion work is done and all added heat changes internal energy. In an isobaric process, part of the heat also provides expansion work, giving a larger heat requirement for the same ideal-gas temperature rise.

For an ideal gas at constant pressure, pΔV=nRΔT. Applying the first law gives the molar heat-capacity relation C_P−C_V=R. This relation is specific to an ideal gas; it is not a universal identity for every material.

Isochoric and isobaric heating
\[ Q_V=nC_V\Delta T,\quad Q_P=nC_P\Delta T,\quad W_P=nR\Delta T,\quad C_P-C_V=R \]
Symbols and assumptions: Q_V/Q_P heat added at fixed volume/pressure (J); n amount (mol); C_V/C_P molar capacities (J/mol K); ΔT temperature rise (K); W_P gas work (J); R gas constant. Assume ideal gas and constant capacities over the interval.
Watch the assumption

Isochoric means fixed volume; it does not mean fixed temperature or zero heat.

Worked example

One mole of monatomic ideal gas warms by 20 K at fixed pressure. Find Q, W and ΔU in terms of R.

Show solution

C_P=5R/2 and C_V=3R/2. Thus Q=50R J, W=20R J and ΔU=30R J; Q−W=ΔU.

Check the idea

4 Core

Isothermal processes and free expansion

An ideal gas undergoing an isothermal process has unchanged internal energy because temperature is fixed. Heat and work can still be nonzero. For reversible expansion, heat from a reservoir replaces the energy transferred as work.

Free expansion into vacuum is different: the external pressure is zero, so boundary work is zero. If also insulated, Q=0 and ΔU=0. An ideal gas then has the same final temperature, but the irreversible process must not be assigned the reversible logarithmic work.

Isothermal processes and free expansion
\[ W_{\rm rev}=nRT\ln\frac{V_f}{V_i},\quad Q=W_{\rm rev},\quad \Delta U=0\quad\text{(ideal isothermal)} \]
Symbols and assumptions: W_rev reversible isothermal work (J); n amount (mol); R gas constant; T fixed absolute temperature (K); V_i/V_f volumes (m³); ln is natural logarithm. Its argument is dimensionless. Q=W applies to the ideal isothermal energy balance.
Watch the assumption

Constant temperature does not mean no heat transfer; insulation means no heat transfer.

Worked example

One mole expands reversibly at 300 K to twice its volume. Find work using R=8.31.

Show solution

W=1×8.31×300×ln2≈1728 J. ΔU=0, so the same heat must enter from the reservoir.

Check the idea

5 Core

Adiabatic change and connected-cylinder reasoning

Adiabatic means no heat crosses the boundary. A rapid expansion or good insulation can approximately achieve it. Expansion work then comes from internal energy and lowers the temperature of an ideal gas; compression raises it.

The pV^γ relation requires a reversible quasistatic adiabatic ideal-gas process. In connected rigid insulated cylinders, choose both gases as the system: no external boundary work and no external heat imply total internal-energy conservation. A valve opening is generally irreversible, so do not apply pV^γ to each side during mixing.

Adiabatic change and connected-cylinder reasoning
\[ pV^\gamma=\text{constant},\quad TV^{\gamma-1}=\text{constant},\quad W=\frac{p_iV_i-p_fV_f}{\gamma-1},\quad U_{1i}+U_{2i}=U_{1f}+U_{2f}\ \text{(isolated rigid pair)} \]
Symbols and assumptions: p pressure (Pa), V volume (m³), T temperature (K), γ=C_P/C_V dimensionless; W work by gas (J), U gas internal energies (J); i/f initial/final. First three require a reversible adiabatic ideal gas with constant γ. The last requires a rigid insulated combined system without other energy exchanges.
Watch the assumption

A connecting valve does not by itself make a process reversible or isothermal. Specify insulation, wall motion and gas species.

Worked example

Two rigid insulated vessels contain the same ideal gas: 1 mol at 300 K and 1 mol at 500 K. After connecting and equilibration, find final temperature.

Show solution

For equal constant C_V, total energy conservation gives C_V(300+500)=2C_V T_f, hence T_f=400 K. Final common pressure then uses the total volume in pV=nRT.

Check the idea

6 Core

Cycles, heat engines and efficiency

A thermodynamic cycle returns to its initial state, so the net internal-energy change is zero. Net heat input equals net work output. On a p–V plot a clockwise loop represents positive net work by the gas.

A heat engine absorbs energy from a hot source, delivers some as work, and rejects the remainder to a colder sink. Efficiency uses work divided by heat absorbed from the hot source, not divided by the net heat input.

Cycles, heat engines and efficiency
\[ \Delta U_{\rm cycle}=0,\quad W=Q_h-Q_c,\quad \eta=\frac{W}{Q_h}=1-\frac{Q_c}{Q_h} \]
Symbols and assumptions: W net cycle work (J); Q_h positive heat absorbed from hot source (J); Q_c positive magnitude rejected to cold sink (J); η dimensionless efficiency. The engine returns to its starting state after each cycle.
Watch the assumption

Q_c is defined here as a positive magnitude; heat-added signs in a process equation must still be handled consistently.

Worked example

An engine takes 800 J from a source and rejects 500 J per cycle. Find work and efficiency.

Show solution

W=800−500=300 J. Efficiency is 300/800=0.375=37.5%.

Check the idea

7 Core

Second law, Carnot limits and refrigerators

The first law conserves energy but does not decide which processes occur spontaneously. The second law rules out converting heat from a single reservoir entirely into cyclic work and requires external work to pump heat from cold to hot.

A reversible Carnot engine sets the maximum efficiency between two temperatures. Real engines have irreversible losses. A refrigerator’s coefficient of performance compares heat removed from the cold region with supplied work; it can exceed one because it measures moved heat, not an energy-conversion fraction.

Second law, Carnot limits and refrigerators
\[ \eta_C=1-\frac{T_c}{T_h},\qquad COP=\frac{Q_c}{W},\qquad COP_C=\frac{T_c}{T_h-T_c} \]
Symbols and assumptions: T_h/T_c hot/cold reservoir temperatures (K), T_h>T_c>0; η_C maximum reversible engine efficiency; Q_c heat removed from cold reservoir (J); W input work (J); COP coefficient of performance; COP_C reversible refrigerator limit. Kelvin is essential.
Watch the assumption

An efficiency fraction cannot exceed one; refrigerator COP can because work helps move additional heat.

Worked example

A Carnot engine operates between 600 and 300 K. Find maximum efficiency. A reversible refrigerator between the same temperatures has what COP?

Show solution

η_C=1−300/600=0.5=50%. Refrigerator COP_C=300/(600−300)=1.

Check the idea
