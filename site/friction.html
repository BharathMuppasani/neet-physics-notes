Laws of Motion · Part 2: Friction

Friction responds to the tendency to slip. Decide whether contact is static or sliding before substituting a coefficient.

← All chaptersPractise this chapter →
1 Core

Static friction is adjustable

Static friction prevents relative slipping at the contact. It adjusts to the amount needed for equilibrium, up to a maximum value. The coefficient sets the limit; it does not force friction to equal that limit in every stationary situation.

To solve, first assume no slip, find the friction required from the equations, and check whether it is within μₛN. If the requirement exceeds the limit, slipping begins and the static assumption fails.

Static friction is adjustable
\[ 0\leq |f_s|\leq\mu_sN,\qquad f_{s,\max}=\mu_sN \]
Symbols and assumptions: fₛ is signed static friction along the contact (N); μₛ is dimensionless static coefficient; N is normal force (N). Friction opposes the relative sliding tendency, not necessarily the centre-of-mass velocity.
Watch the assumption

At rest with no horizontal force and no slip tendency, friction can be zero.

Worked example

A block has normal force 40 N and μₛ=0.5. A 12 N horizontal force is applied. Find static friction.

Show solution

The maximum available static friction is 20 N. Since only 12 N is required to balance the push, friction is 12 N opposite it and the block remains at rest.

Check the idea

2 Core

Kinetic friction and work

Once surfaces slide relative to each other, kinetic friction opposes that relative motion. In the elementary model its magnitude is approximately μₖN. It generally converts organised mechanical energy into internal energy.

Find the normal force first, including any vertical push or pull. Then use the sliding-force equation along the motion. The kinetic coefficient is often smaller than the static coefficient, though material and speed dependence are more complicated in reality.

Kinetic friction and work
\[ f_k=\mu_kN,\qquad W_f=-f_ks\quad\text{(fixed surface, opposite sliding)} \]
Symbols and assumptions: fₖ is kinetic friction magnitude (N); μₖ is dimensionless kinetic coefficient; N normal force (N); s is sliding distance (m); W_f is work on the sliding body (J) for constant opposing friction on a stationary surface.
Watch the assumption

Friction can do positive work on a body carried by a moving belt; use the actual force and ground-frame displacement.

Worked example

A 2 kg block slides on a level surface with μₖ=0.2. Use g=10. Find deceleration and work over 3 m.

Show solution

N=20 N and fₖ=4 N. Acceleration opposes motion with magnitude 4/2=2 m/s². Work is −4×3=−12 J.

Check the idea

3 Core

Inclines, angle of friction and angle of repose

On an incline, gravity has a downhill component and normal force is reduced to mg cosθ if no other perpendicular force acts. Static friction must supply mg sinθ to hold the block. Their ratio determines the limiting angle.

The angle of repose is the largest slope angle allowing rest in this model. The angle of friction describes the tilt of the combined normal-and-limiting-friction reaction away from the normal. Both have tangent μₛ at the slipping threshold.

Inclines, angle of friction and angle of repose
\[ \tan\theta_r=\mu_s,\qquad a=g(\sin\theta-\mu_k\cos\theta)\quad\text{(sliding downhill)} \]
Symbols and assumptions: θᵣ is repose angle; θ actual incline angle; μₛ and μₖ static/kinetic coefficients; a is downhill acceleration (m/s²); g gravity. The acceleration formula assumes downhill sliding, no other forces and maintained contact.
Watch the assumption

Friction points uphill for downhill sliding, but can point downhill if an external pull tends to move the block uphill.

Worked example

A block has μₛ=1/√3 on an incline. Find its angle of repose.

Show solution

The limiting condition mg sinθ=μₛmg cosθ gives tanθ=1/√3, so θᵣ=30°.

Check the idea

4 Core

Pulling at an angle and maximum available friction

An upward-angled pull partially supports the block and reduces the normal force. A downward-angled push increases it. Because the friction limit depends on normal force, changing the force direction also changes the threshold for sliding.

At impending motion on a level floor, horizontal pull balances limiting friction while vertical forces balance. Solve both together rather than using N=mg. If the normal force reaches zero, the assumed contact model no longer applies.

Pulling at an angle and maximum available friction
\[ N=mg-P\sin\theta,\qquad P\cos\theta=\mu_s(mg-P\sin\theta) \]
Symbols and assumptions: P is pull magnitude (N), θ angle above horizontal; m mass (kg), g gravity (m/s²); N normal force (N); μₛ static coefficient. Equations describe impending horizontal slip with N≥0 and no vertical acceleration.
Watch the assumption

Using μmg without checking vertical force balance misses the main effect of an angled pull.

Worked example

A 10 kg block is pulled with a 20 N upward vertical component while g=10. If μₛ=0.5, find the horizontal friction limit.

Show solution

The normal force is 100−20=80 N. Maximum static friction is 0.5×80=40 N.

Check the idea

5 Core

Friction in connected bodies and turning

When stacked blocks move together, static friction can accelerate the upper block. Test whether the required ma is below μₛN. A level-road turn uses the same idea: static friction accelerates the vehicle sideways without sideways sliding.

Choose the system to eliminate internal friction when finding common acceleration, then isolate the body that needs friction to determine the no-slip limit. Do not assume that friction on two touching bodies has the same direction; their friction forces are opposite.

Friction in connected bodies and turning
\[ a_{\max}=\mu_sg\quad\text{(upper block)},\qquad v_{\max}=\sqrt{\mu_srg}\quad\text{(level turn)} \]
Symbols and assumptions: a_max is maximum no-slip acceleration (m/s²) for a horizontally carried block with N=mg; v_max maximum turn speed (m/s); r radius (m); μₛ static coefficient; g gravity. The road relation assumes level motion and no other lateral force.
Watch the assumption

Static friction can point in the direction of a body’s acceleration and do positive work on it.

Worked example

A top block is carried by a lower block accelerating at 3 m/s². With μₛ=0.4 and g=10, will it slip?

Show solution

The no-slip limit is μₛg=4 m/s². Required acceleration is 3, so static friction can carry it without slipping.

Check the idea
