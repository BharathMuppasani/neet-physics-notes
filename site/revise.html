CHAPTER 8

Mechanical Properties of Solids

Stress\( \sigma = \dfrac{F}{A} \)N m⁻² = Pa, dimensions [ML⁻¹T⁻²]. Internal restoring force per area.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Strains\( \dfrac{\Delta L}{L},\quad \theta \approx \dfrac{\Delta x}{L},\quad \dfrac{\Delta V}{V} \)Longitudinal, shear, volumetric. Dimensionless.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Hooke's law\( \text{stress} = E \times \text{strain} \)Valid only up to the proportional limit. Modulus has stress's units.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Young's modulus\( Y = \dfrac{FL}{A\,\Delta L} \Rightarrow \Delta L = \dfrac{FL}{\pi r^2 Y} \)Solids only. Steel ≈ 2×10¹¹ Pa. Rigid body: Y = ∞.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Shear modulus\( G = \dfrac{F/A}{\theta} \)Solids only. Usually G ≈ Y/3.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Bulk modulus\( B = -\dfrac{p}{\Delta V/V},\quad k = \dfrac1B \)Solids, liquids, gases. Water 2.2×10⁹ Pa. Fractional change in radius = p/3B.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Poisson's ratio\( \sigma = -\dfrac{\Delta d/d}{\Delta L/L} \)Most conventional materials: 0 to 0.5; auxetic materials can have negative ratios. \( Y = 2G(1+\sigma) = 3B(1-2\sigma) \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Elastic energy\( U = \tfrac12 F\Delta L = \dfrac{YA(\Delta L)^2}{2L} \)Per volume: \( u = \tfrac12\,\text{stress}\times\text{strain} = \dfrac{\text{stress}^2}{2Y} \). Mass M stretching wire by l: U = Mgl/2.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Wire as a spring\( k = \dfrac{YA}{L} \)Series: \( 1/k = 1/k_1 + 1/k_2 \). Parallel: \( k = k_1 + k_2 \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Own weight\( \Delta L = \dfrac{MgL}{2AY} = \dfrac{\rho g L^2}{2Y} \)Half of what the same weight hung at the end would produce.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Thermal stress\( F = YA\alpha\,\Delta T \)Rod clamped between rigid walls. Independent of length.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Beam bending\( \delta = \dfrac{W\ell^3}{4bd^3Y} \)Doubling depth d cuts sag 8×, which is why I-beams are deep.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →

Symbol guide: F and W are force magnitudes in N; M is mass in kg; A is cross-sectional area in m²; L or ℓ is length in m; Y, G and B are moduli in Pa. σ denotes stress in the stress card and Poisson's ratio in the Poisson card. Δ means a signed change; p in the compression formulas means the added pressure. In beam bending, b is breadth, d is vertical depth and δ is sag.

Viscosity & terminal velocity

Newton's law\( F = \eta A \dfrac{dv}{dx} \)η in Pa·s; 1 poise = 0.1 Pa·s. [ML⁻¹T⁻¹]. Ideal fluid: η = 0.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Stokes' law\( F = 6\pi\eta r v \)∝ r and ∝ v (so ∝ A^½).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Terminal velocity\( v_t = \dfrac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta} \)∝ r² and ∝ (ρ − σ). Rising bubble: \( v = 2r^2\sigma g/9\eta \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
n drops merge\( v' = n^{2/3} v \)8 drops → 4v. One sphere broken into n: each gets \( v/n^{2/3} \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
At terminal velocity\( F_\text{viscous} = W - F_B \)If liquid density = ½ body density: viscous force = Mg/2.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →

Surface tension & capillarity

Surface tension\( T = \dfrac{F}{L} = \dfrac{W}{\Delta A} \)N/m = J/m². Scalar. Salt raises it, soap lowers it.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Film on a frame\( F = 2TL \)Two surfaces. Square frame: 8TL. Ring/disc with hole: 2π(R+r)T.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Work on film / bubble\( W = 2T\Delta A,\ \ W_\text{bubble} = 8\pi T(r_2^2 - r_1^2) \)Drop (one surface): \( 4\pi T(r_2^2 - r_1^2) \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Drops merging\( E = 4\pi r^2T\,(n - n^{2/3}) \)Energy released (area falls). Splitting needs energy.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Excess pressure\( \text{drop } \dfrac{2T}{R},\ \text{bubble } \dfrac{4T}{R},\ \text{air bubble } \dfrac{2T}{R} \)Air bubble at depth h: \( P_\text{in} = P_0 + \rho g h + 2T/r \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Two bubbles joined\( r = \dfrac{r_1 r_2}{r_2 - r_1} \)Interface radius. Small bubble shrinks into the big one.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →
Capillary rise\( h = \dfrac{2T\cos\theta}{r\rho g} \)r = inner radius. hr = const. Mass raised ∝ r: \( mg = 2\pi rT\cos\theta \).Symbols, assumptions and explanation →

Error sum is a first-order maximum estimate with ρ and g exact.Symbols, assumptions and explanation →

FROM YOUR ✗ MARKS

Traps you have already met

Area ∝ d². Hydraulic press 1.4 cm and 7 cm: ratio 25, not 5 (Test-05 Q5).
Pressure has no direction. Only the force it produces does (Test-05 Q3).
Coin falls off a block: block rises, water level falls (Test-05 Q12).
Variable density: integrate for the average; don't take the middle value (Test-05 Q13).
Buoyancy in an ordinary hydrostatic liquid equals the displaced liquid’s weight; the gravity-driven contribution vanishes in ideal free fall (Test-05 Q15).
Steady flow means the velocity at a point is fixed, not each particle's velocity (Test-05 Q16).
Bernoulli: P + ρgh + ½ρv². Only the KE term has ½. It is energy conservation (Q21, Q42).
Max jet range is at h = H/2 and equals H (Test-05 Q30).
Drops merging release energy, not mass → energy (Test-06 Q7).
Capillary rise depends on the inner radius only (Test-06 Q16).
Terminal velocity ∝ r², not r (Test-06 Q24).
Count every edge: disc with hole 2π(R+r)T, frame 8lT (Test-06 Q25).
Surface tension is a scalar (Test-06 Q27).
Lubricant needs enough viscosity to maintain its film (Test-06 Q43); operating conditions determine the practical choice.
